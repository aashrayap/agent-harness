# Unslop patterns

Use this catalog as a diagnostic, not a replacement style contract. Fix only
patterns that weaken the actual prose.

## Content

1. **Puffery.** Cut claims such as "pivotal moment," "testament to," or
   "evolving landscape." State what happened.
2. **Name-dropping.** Do not list outlets or authorities without relevant
   context. Name the source and what it established.
3. **Superficial `-ing` phrases.** Delete or substantiate dangling claims such
   as "highlighting," "ensuring," or "showcasing."
4. **Promotional language.** Replace "groundbreaking," "renowned," and similar
   sales copy with supported descriptions.
5. **Vague attribution.** Name the expert, report, or critic, or remove the
   claim.
6. **Formulaic challenge arcs.** Replace "despite challenges" narratives with
   the specific constraint and result.

## Language

7. Prefer plain words over stock AI vocabulary such as "delve," "pivotal,"
   "tapestry," "underscore," and abstract "landscape."
8. Replace fancy forms of "is" or "has" such as "serves as," "stands as," and
   "boasts."
9. State the point directly instead of "not just X, but Y."
10. Do not force a rule of three. Use the natural number of items.
11. Pick one accurate noun instead of cycling synonyms.
12. Avoid false "from X to Y" ranges when no scale connects the endpoints.

## Style and communication artifacts

13. Avoid em-dash overuse. Prefer a period or comma when the format allows it.
14. Use colons for lists or examples, not as a default sentence connector.
15. Do not bold every proper noun or acronym.
16. Remove inline-header lists whose label repeats the sentence.
17. Use sentence-case headings unless the governing template says otherwise.
18. Remove decorative emoji unless the requested style requires them.
19. Preserve quotation style when it is source-locked; otherwise prefer the
   document's established quote style.
20. Cut chatbot phrases such as "I hope this helps" and "Let me know if."
21. Replace cutoff disclaimers with sourced limits or remove the claim.
22. Remove sycophancy and answer directly.

## Filler, jargon, and plain speech

23. Shorten filler: "in order to" becomes "to"; "due to the fact that" becomes
   "because"; delete "it is important to note."
24. Collapse stacked hedges to the smallest accurate uncertainty marker.
25. Replace generic conclusions with specific facts, decisions, or next steps.
26. Replace abstract metaphor nouns with the mechanism's real name.
27. Say what a thing does. If a sentence could describe any project unchanged,
   make it concrete or cut it.
28. Split sentences that require backtracking. Prefer one idea per sentence.
29. Prefer active voice when the actor matters and is known.
30. Cut adverbs that prop up weak verbs; use the measured result when known.
31. Prefer the plain word: "use" over "utilize" or "leverage," "help" over
   "facilitate," and "if" over "in the event that."
