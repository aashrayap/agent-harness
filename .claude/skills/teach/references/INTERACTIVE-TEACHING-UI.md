# Interactive teaching surfaces

Make it obvious where to act, what the action changes, and what stays fixed.
This is control–display mapping, supported by proximity and common-region grouping.

## Group by what drives the content

Before arranging the page, list each region and the inputs that change it.
Regions with different inputs need distinguishable boundaries:

- Guided steps belong with the illustration and explanation they advance.
- Scenario controls belong with the chart and feedback they update.
- Stable references belong in a separate labeled region. Exploring a reference
  should not silently change the guided step or scenario.

Use whitespace and headings first; add containers or tabs when needed. Do not
rely on color alone. A chart that ignores the selected guided moment should not
look like that moment's output. Label its scope, or move it into its own activity.
Preserve each activity's state when switching views. Name resets by their scope.

Keep controls and their results close at narrow widths too. When both cannot
remain visible, provide useful local feedback and a clear route to the result.
Use native labels, current values and units, fieldsets for related inputs, visible
keyboard focus, accessible chart descriptions, and text that explains the result.

Separate timescales when an activity mixes immediate effects and sustained
changes. Label illustrative models at the output; avoid invented personal
precision. A fixed comparison should state its assumptions and remain fixed.

## Verify and learn

Exercise each control and check the intended display changes while unrelated
regions retain their state. Check narrow-screen layout and keyboard operation.
A successful click is evidence of function, not evidence of learning.

When requested, record a short observation → design change → observed outcome
note. Keep untested teaching hypotheses distinct from learner feedback. Do not
create a learner database, health tracker, or recurring automation from this rule.
